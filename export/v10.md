## The real problem: this currently looks like a generic AI-generated SaaS template

Be specific about what to avoid, because the last pass reproduced this pattern almost exactly:

**Avoid these specific patterns** (all currently present and all overused across countless AI-built SaaS pages right now):
- A small pill-shaped "eyebrow" badge sitting above every section heading (e.g. "SIMPLE 3-STEP SETUP," "BUILT FOR SPEED & CONTROL," "TRANSPARENT PRICING")
- A vague floating gradient orb/blob decoration in the hero
- Numbered "01 / 02 / 03" step cards in identical bordered boxes
- Every section following the same rhythm: badge → centered heading → centered subtext → grid of identical bordered cards
- A near-all-dark-navy background used for almost every section, with yellow only as small accent dots/borders
- Thin, low-contrast borders on translucent dark cards as the primary visual texture throughout

**Do this instead:**
- **Commit to real light sections.** At least half the page should be white/light background, not dark — use dark sections deliberately for 2–3 high-impact moments (e.g. the hero, and one "big statement" section later), not as the default for everything.
- **Break the rhythm.** No two consecutive sections should use the same layout pattern (badge+heading+card-grid). Vary it: one section could be a large asymmetric two-column layout, another could use big oversized typography as the visual element itself (no card, no icon — just very large, confident text), another could use a full-width image/illustration break.
- **Replace the generic floating-card hero mockup** with something more specific to this product — an actual styled illustration or mockup of a WhatsApp order message thread, or a stylized illustration of a shop owner and their phone, rather than an abstract "app UI floating in space" graphic.
- **Use yellow boldly in at least one section**, not just as a thin accent — e.g. a full yellow-background section for a bold callout or the final CTA, with black text on it (this is a genuinely distinctive, on-brand move most SaaS templates don't make, and it uses the palette you asked for properly).
- **Vary card/section shapes** — not everything needs a rounded-rectangle bordered card on a dark background. Try some sections with no card container at all, just well-composed type and whitespace.
- **Typography as a design element**: use at least one moment where large-scale type itself carries the visual weight of a section (oversized numerals, an oversized quote, an oversized statistic) instead of relying on icons and cards for visual interest everywhere.
